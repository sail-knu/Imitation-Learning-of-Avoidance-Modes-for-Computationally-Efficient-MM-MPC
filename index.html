Imitation Learning of Avoidance Modesfor Computationally Efficient Multimodal MPC

A graph network learns the avoidance side of each obstacle from a multimodal MPC expert, cutting sixteen OCPs per control period to at most two while the safety constraints stay in the optimization.

Why multimodal MPC is expensive

Every combination of avoidance sides is its own OCP

With TC-CBF constraints, each obstacle is passed on a fixed side, so the expert solves one OCP per side combination: K = 2Nb per control period.

Arbitrate

The expert's rule keeps the previous mode unless the proposal is clearly cheaper.

  • One solve when the proposal matches the previous mode.
  • The network never outputs a control input.
  • At most two OCPs, however many branch obstacles.
Graph neural network

Edges carry the gap the vehicle will actually meet

A neighbor can close the gap an isolated side choice would use, so SideNet links the branch obstacles in a complete graph with pair-gap edges.

NODES

One slot per obstacle

Relative position, velocity and radius, plus the ego state. Permutation-equivariant.

EDGES

Gap at the encounter

Current gap, gap at the encounter time, and closing rate.

SIZE

Small and fast

94,851 parameters, 0.4 ms per forward pass on one core.

Conclusion

A learned mode decision without a learned control input

What the study shows

  • Two OCPs instead of sixteen, at 93% of the expert's success.
  • Every executed input comes from a TC-CBF constrained OCP.
  • Pair-gap edges, not extra capacity, carry the side decision.

Next steps

  • Extend the graph from the branch obstacles to the full selected set.
  • Validate the controller on embedded hardware.
